- **Structured Contrast Against Round 16 + Addendum B:**
  1. **Observational Decomposition vs. Causal Input Intervention:** Molina & Zhang fit an observational four-component ANOVA (`delta = mu + alpha_c + beta_p + gamma_{c,p}`, `decomposition/anova.py`) to measured responses across the 4 Replogle/Nadig CRISPRi screens (`k562, rpe1, hepg2, jurkat`), inspect control-PC capture of template vs. residual (`fig1_e`), and decompose saved predictions of `STATE` (`arc-state==0.10.2`, source-only transfer), `MORPH`, `scGPT`, `BioLORD`, `scGen`, and DepMap `Ridge`/`MLP` (`fig4_b`). Their GitHub code contains **no frozen-checkpoint cross-cell-line input control-cell swap**, **no `Occam` evaluation**, **no TOST equivalence test ($\delta = 0.04$) or within-line disjoint control resampling**, **no `FCT` positive control**, and **no post-2025 cross-study screens (`a549_ko`, `dld1`, `cd4t_rest`)**.
  2. **Explicit Reconciliation of the Tension Between Molina & Zhang (`"cannot be recovered from control expression"`) and Round 12 `K1` (`FCT` Target Controls Beat Mismatched Controls on `a549_ko`):**
     At face value, Molina & Zhang's abstract claim (*"the perturbation and cell-line specific components are high-dimensional and cannot be recovered from control expression"*) sits in direct tension with Round 12 `K1`, where closed-form `FCT` using target `a549_ko` control expression outperformed every mismatched cell-line control on `r_orth_cal` (`+0.1898`, minimum paired advantage `+0.0796` vs. `nadig_jurkat`). Both results can coexist — while revealing a sharp boundary where a strong reading of Molina & Zhang would conflict:
     - **(a) What `"recover from control expression"` means (Control-PC prediction vs. baseline-gated multiplicative fold-change interaction):** Molina & Zhang ask whether the high-dimensional additive residual $\gamma_{c,p}$ can be recovered linearly from **unperturbed control-expression PCs** (`fig1_e: Control-PC capture of template and residual`) across the 4 Replogle/Nadig lines. By contrast, `FCT` does **not** predict $\gamma_{c,p}$ from control expression alone: it pairs **each perturbation's source multiplicative fold change $\overline{\mathrm{FC}}_{p,g}$** with the **target cell line's gene-level control mean $\mu_{t,g}$** via $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{FCT}}_{t,p,g} = \log(1 + \mu_{t,g}\overline{\mathrm{FC}}_{p,g}) - \log(1 + \mu_{t,g}) \approx w(\mu_{t,g})\log\overline{\mathrm{FC}}_{p,g}$ ($w(\mu)=\mu/(1+\mu)$). Even a completely conserved multiplicative knockdown produces cell-line-specific additive `log1p` deltas whenever baseline expression $\mu_{t,g}$ varies across cell lines (an unexpressed gene cannot be downregulated). That multiplicative baseline-gating effect lies inside the additive interaction term $\gamma_{c,p}$ (and survives orthogonalization against additive `Transfer_Mean` in `r_orth_cal`), yet requires the **product** of target control expression and perturbation fold change — not control PCs alone.
     - **(b) Metric, orthogonalization, and variance scale:** Molina & Zhang's primary metric `pref = corr(delta - T_train, delta_hat - T_train)` subtracts a single training-perturbation template vector $T_{\mathrm{train}}$ (analogous to Systema's centroid shift), whereas Round 16's `r_orth_cal` projects out each perturbation's own cross-line average `Transfer_Mean` $\bar{\Delta}^{\mathrm{src}}_p$ and disattenuates by `rho_ab_orth`. Moreover, `FCT`'s confirmed `r_orth_cal = +0.1898` on `a549_ko` accounts for $r^2 \approx 3.6\%$ of disattenuated orthogonal variance — statistically decisive against mismatched controls yet confirming that `>95%` of the high-dimensional cell-line-specific residual remains unrecovered by baseline expression gating alone.
     - **(c) Residual empirical conflict:** If Molina & Zhang's statement is interpreted strictly to mean that target-line control expression carries **zero** recoverable information about cell-line-specific perturbation residuals beyond the global/cell-line template, then Round 12 `K1` (`FCT` beating mismatched controls on `a549_ko` by $\ge +0.0796$ on `r_orth_cal`) **directly conflicts** with that strong claim — and motivates Round 16 + Addendum B's core audit question: given that closed-form `FCT` demonstrably extracts recoverable target-control signal on `r_orth_cal`, do frozen `STATE_SE` and `Occam` extract it too?

### §2.2 COMPASS — Liang & Singh (2026) (`P1.1`, Citing Molina & Zhang 2026)
- **Exact Citation:** Huan Liang & Rohit Singh (Duke University), *"COMPASS: Component-Wise Inference of Shared and Gene-Specific Perturbation Response"*, `bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.08.03.742643` (v1 `2026-08-06`, `CC BY-NC-ND`, Europe PMC `PPR1293149`). *(bioRxiv full HTML `未能读取`, §5.)*
- **Directly Verified Verbatim Quote (bioRxiv REST API abstract):**
  > *"We formalize this structure with COMPASS, an interpretable linear model that decomposes each response into shared and gene-specific components and estimates them separately. The shared-response component is modeled as a cell-line-wide response scaled by a perturbation-specific coefficient. This coefficient is strongly conserved across cell lines. The residual gene-specific component--which is moderately conserved across cell lines--recovers pathway-level programs. COMPASS outperforms scGPT, CPA, GEARS, GenePert, and [STATE] in both response accuracy (de-biased Pearson delta 0.34 vs. <= 0.32) and perturbation discrimination (cosine PDS gain 0.23 vs. <= 0.08)."* *(Note: bioRxiv XML small-caps tag `SO_SCPLOWTATEC_SCPLOW` renders as `STATE`.)*
- **Classification & Distinction from Round 16 + Addendum B:** COMPASS is an interpretable two-component linear baseline showing conserved perturbation-scaled shared structure across 2,270 CRISPRi perturbations in 6 cell lines and benchmarking against `STATE`, `scGPT`, `CPA`, `GEARS`, and `GenePert`. It is a **modeling/decomposition study** (similar in spirit to Molina & Zhang 2026 and BiPert `2611.31227`), NOT a frozen-checkpoint cross-cell-line input control-swap audit of `STATE_SE` and `Occam` with TOST $\delta = 0.04$ and within-line disjoint resampling.
