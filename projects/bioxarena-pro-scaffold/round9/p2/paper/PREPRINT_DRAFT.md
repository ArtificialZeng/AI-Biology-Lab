## Abstract

Predicting cell-line-specific CRISPRi responses solely from target-line control cells remains unresolved, with Arc Virtual Cell Challenge (VCC) 2026 top models reaching slopes of `0.02–0.13` versus `1.02` for replicates. We conducted a pre-registered, leakage-locked leave-one-line-out zero-shot evaluation across six human Perturb-seq datasets (`1,419,744` cells, `6,148` genes), isolating two development screens (`Replogle` `K562`, `RPE1`) and `KOLF2.1J` (`N = 18`) from a three-line held-out confirmatory cohort (`nadig_hepg2`, `nadig_jurkat`, and `vcc2025_h1` with only `N = 80` shared reproducible targets).

A single per-line scalar (`Transfer_Mean_GlobalScale`) produces raw residual correlations of `-0.126` to `+0.250` on confirmatory lines (`-0.316` to `+0.352` across all six lines), collapsing to `0.000` after per-perturbation shared-amplitude orthogonalization (`r_orth_cal`). Under `r_orth_cal`, Bonferroni `97.5%` confidence intervals, and `3/3` unanimity, **both confirmatory groups were NOT CONFIRMED**: Group A (`Hybrid_ProjShared`, co-fluctuation transfer) did not confirm `H-A1` (`slope > 0.13`: `0/3`), `H-A2` (`r_orth_cal > 0`: `2/3`, failing on `H1`), `H-A3` (mismatched-covariance specificity: `0/3`), or `H-A4` (`3/3` inferior to `Transfer_Mean`); Group B (`Transfer_Mean_VarScaled`) did not confirm `H-B1` (`0/3`), `H-B2` (`2/3`, failing on `H1`), `H-B3` (mismatched-variance and `100`-permutation nulls: `0/3`), or `H-B4` (`3/3` inferior). In this dataset, co-fluctuation structure exhibits no detectable increment beyond marginal variance (`R14`), and gains over `Transfer_Mean` may be explained by target-line marginal gene variance. Independent recomputations by `A-T2`, `A-T3`, and master controller `Claude` (Group B `r_orth_cal`) agreed identically (`264/264` deterministic `A-T2`/`A-T3` checks `<= 1.11e-14`; `8/8` hypothesis consensus). These results suggest that amplitude orthogonalization and matched-control nulls should be applied before claiming zero-shot specificity.

## 1. Introduction

Predicting how an unseen human cell type responds transcriptionally to CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) when only unperturbed control cells are available in that target line—zero-shot cross-cell-line generalization—is a central test for computational virtual cell models (Adduri et al., 2026; Molina & Zhang, 2026; Kawsar et al., 2026). Recent large-scale benchmarking reveals a stark asymmetry between recovering *conserved* perturbation responses and recovering *cell-line-specific* deviations from source-line averages. In the Arc Institute Virtual Cell Challenge (VCC) 2026 Wrap-Up (`2026-11-19`), even top-performing submissions achieved high cross-line cosine agreement (`0.77–0.89`, vs. `0.55` measured), yet yielded pooled line-specific slopes of only `0.02–0.13` compared with `1.02` for biological replicates, leading Arc to conclude that the line-specific component remains largely missing. Parallel studies reinforce how easily zero-shot difficulty is masked: `CASTER` (Kawsar et al., 2026) showed that at `0%` target-line perturbations, zero-shot models trail simple perturbation means and require `2.5%–30%` target perturbation screening to surpass them; Molina & Zhang (2026) found that additive cell-line control means poorly recover perturbation-by-line interaction residuals $\gamma_{c,p}$ in linear regression; and Shoeibi & Yousefi (2026, `arXiv:2608.00152v3`) demonstrated under a locked, blinded, pre-registered evaluation protocol that frozen Geneformer representations carrying in-distribution predictive signal on VCC ($\Delta R^2 = +0.1645$) fail zero-shot transfer across external CRISPRi screens (`Spearman rho = -0.139` and `-0.267`, below random-feature controls), while uncovering strong entanglement between VCC aggregate endpoints and perturbation cell count (`n_cells`, linear count-only $R^2 = +0.4325$).

In single-cell perturbation modeling, `CIPHER` (Kuznets-Speck et al., 2025) demonstrated via fluctuation-dissipation linear response theory that single-cell control gene-gene covariance $C_{t,:,g}/C_{t,g,g}$ tracks downstream transcriptional responses within a biological context when scaled by a target-fitted response parameter $\hat{a}_g$. However, transferring such target-line control statistics strictly *zero-shot* across distinct cell lines introduces three methodological hazards that motivated the present pre-registered study:

1. **Un-orthogonalized whole-context scalar amplitude confounding (`R18`):** Evaluating raw residuals $\Delta^{\mathrm{obs}}_{t,p} - \hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_p$ against model predictions leaves global cell-line response-gain mismatches $(\beta_t - 1)\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_p$ inside both target and prediction residuals. As demonstrated in `CGC` (Huang et al., 2026) and `AMM-SimWMag` (Sun et al., 2026) for Tier-1 global scalar amplitude separation ($T - \beta_t S$), failing to remove whole-context amplitude allows a trivial single-scalar multiplier to generate large spurious residual correlations.
2. **Marginal variance versus true gene-gene co-fluctuation (`R4`, `R14`):** Target-line control covariance diagonals ($C_{t,gg}$) reflect baseline gene dynamic range and variance heterogeneity rather than higher-order network wiring (`off-diagonal` co-fluctuation). Prior variance- or expression-aware predictors—including `Illumina/BioInsight CellularAI` (VCC 2026 #1), `AtlasShift` (VCC 2026 #82), and `CASTER`—already exploit target baseline control expression statistics, leaving open whether `C_t` off-diagonal structure adds detectable accuracy beyond marginal variance (`Transfer_Mean_VarScaled` / `TMVS`).
3. **Lack of mismatched-control and gene-permutation specificity checks (`R3`, `R12`, `R18`):** Without verifying that matched target-line control statistics outperform mismatched cell lines' control matrices and gene-label permutations, generic scaling properties of highly responsive genes can masquerade as line-specific biological recovery.

Following the locked, blinded pre-registration discipline previously applied to CRISPRi transfer by Shoeibi & Yousefi (2026), we harmonized six large human CRISPRi Perturb-seq atlases onto `6,148` common genes, completed development calibration and pre-registration revisions (`PREREG_A.md` `v1` $\to$ `v4`) exclusively on two `Replogle et al. (2022)` screens before locking an immutable code freeze (`1ca9f60`), and conducted a dual-team confirmatory audit across a three-line held-out cohort (`nadig_hepg2`, `nadig_jurkat`, `vcc2025_h1`). We additionally characterize a three-tier multiplicative gain decomposition (`R19`), contrast it against Molina & Zhang's (2026) additive two-way ANOVA, quantify upward selection bias in top-50 differential-expression noise ceilings (`S6`), and report a clearly demarcated post-hoc exploratory specificity analysis of secondary Fold-Change Transfer (`FCT`) to guide future prospective verification on unseen datasets.

### 2.3 Zero-Shot Predictors and Control-Matched Specificity Nulls

Under LOLO evaluation, when predicting held-out target line $t$, source model state used strictly the other five source lines $\mathcal{S} = \{s \neq t\}$ and their observed perturbation vectors $\Delta^{\mathrm{obs}}_{s,p}$ and control covariances $C_s = \frac{1}{n_s - 1}(X_{s,\mathrm{ctrl}} - \bar{X}_{s,\mathrm{ctrl}})^\top (X_{s,\mathrm{ctrl}} - \bar{X}_{s,\mathrm{ctrl}})$ (primary `--shrink 0.0`; sensitivity `S3` `--shrink 0.001`). Target access was strictly confined to target control cells (covariance $C_t$ or libnorm control mean $\mu_t$) and target gene identities $g_p$ (`src/predictors.py`):

1. **`Transfer_Mean`:** Cross-source average $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_p = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_p|} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_p} \Delta^{\mathrm{obs}}_{s,p}$, where $\mathcal{S}_p \subseteq \mathcal{S}$ are source lines containing target perturbation $p$ (all evaluated targets satisfy $|\mathcal{S}_p| \ge 1$).
2. **`Source_Grand_Mean`:** Constant mean $\bar{b}_S$ across all perturbations in $\mathcal{S}$.
3. **`CIPHER_Linear` (zero-shot adaptation):** $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{CIPHER}}_{t,p} = \hat{\lambda}\, C_{t,:,g_p} / C_{t,g_p,g_p}$ (`min_var = 1e-8`), where global scalar $\hat{\lambda}$ is fit by self-gene-masked ordinary least squares strictly on source lines $\mathcal{S}$. Unlike `CIPHER v2` (Kuznets-Speck et al., 2025), which fits per-perturbation amplitude $\hat{a}_g$ on target perturbations, this adaptation is strictly zero-shot.
4. **`Hybrid_Raw` and Group A Primary `Hybrid_ProjShared`:** Differential CIPHER covariance shift added to `Transfer_Mean`: $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{Hybrid\_Raw}}_{t,p} = \hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_p + (\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{CIPHER}}_{t,p} - \bar{\Delta}^{\mathrm{CIPHER}}_{\mathcal{S}_p,p})$, and primary **`Hybrid_ProjShared`** $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_p + P_{\perp}(\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{CIPHER}}_{t,p} - \bar{\Delta}^{\mathrm{CIPHER}}_{\mathcal{S}_p,p})$, where $P_{\perp} = I - Q_S Q_S^\top$ removes the source-only orthonormal subspace $\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{1}, \bar{b}_S, a_S\}$ ($a_S$: PC1 of normalized off-$\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{1}, \bar{b}_S\}$ source residuals). Sensitivity `S2` tests unprojected `Hybrid_Raw`.
5. **Group B Primary `Transfer_Mean_VarScaled` (`TMVS`):** Parameter-free control marginal variance-ratio rescaling $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMVS}}_{t,p,g} = \hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_{p,g} \sqrt{C_{t,gg} / \bar{C}_{S,gg}}$, where $\bar{C}_{S,gg}$ averages source control variances across $\mathcal{S}$ (`min_var = 1e-8`).
6. **`Transfer_Mean_GlobalScale` (`R18` negative-amplitude callout):** Single line-level scalar rescaling $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{Glob}}_{t,p} = c_{\mathrm{global}} \hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_p$, where $c_{\mathrm{global}} = \sqrt{\overline{\operatorname{diag}(C_t)} / \overline{\operatorname{diag}(\bar{C}_S)}}$.
7. **`Hybrid_on_TMVS` (`R14` co-fluctuation increment):** $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMVS}}_{t,p} + P_{\perp}(\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{CIPHER}}_{t,p} - \bar{\Delta}^{\mathrm{CIPHER}}_{\mathcal{S}_p,p})$.
8. **Secondary Baseline `FCT` (`Fold-Change Transfer`, `R15`):** Geometric mean source library-normalized fold change $\overline{\mathrm{FC}}_{p,g} = \exp\big(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_p|}\sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_p} \ln(\hat{p}_{s,g}/\mu_{s,g})\big)$ with $\hat{p}_{s,g} = \max(\operatorname{expm1}(\operatorname{log1p}(\mu_{s,g}) + \Delta^{\mathrm{obs}}_{s,p,g}), 10^{-8})$, projected onto target control libnorm mean via $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{FCT}}_{t,p,g} = \operatorname{log1p}(\mu_{t,g} \overline{\mathrm{FC}}_{p,g}) - \operatorname{log1p}(\mu_{t,g})$. As established by prior-art auditing (`PRIOR_ART_B.md`, `PRIOR_ART_C.md`), `FCT` is **not a novel method**: its target control-mean fold-change scaling class matches VCC 2026 #1 `Illumina/BioInsight CellularAI` (`fc * 2^L`) and VCC 2026 #82 `AtlasShift` (`control_probability * 2^effect`).
9. **Specificity & Mechanism Negative Controls:** (i) `NegCtrl_HybridProj_MismatchedCov(s')` replacing $C_t$ with each of the `K-1 = 5` other lines' $C_{s'}$; (ii) `NegCtrl_HybridProj_DiagCov` zeroing off-diagonals of $C_t$; (iii) `NegCtrl_HybridProj_ShuffledGenes` permuting gene labels symmetrically; (iv) `NegCtrl_HybridProj_SrcCorrTgtVar` reconstructing $D_t^{1/2} \bar{R}_S D_t^{1/2}$ from pooled source correlations $\bar{R}_S$ and target marginal variances $D_t$ (`R4`); (v) `NegCtrl_TMVS_MismatchedVar(s')` replacing $C_{t,gg}$ with each of the `5` mismatched lines' $C_{s',gg}$; and (vi) `NegCtrl_TMVS_ShuffledVar` across `100` deterministic gene permutations (`np.random.default_rng(k).permutation(G)` for `k = 0..99`, rule `C2`).

### 2.5 Amplitude-Orthogonalized Evaluation Metrics, CIs, and R19 Decomposition

- **Residual definitions & `r_orth_cal` (`R17`):** For each evaluated perturbation $p$, excluding self-target gene $g_p$, the primary evaluation mask (`resp_top50`) selects the 50 genes with largest $|\Delta^{\mathrm{obs}}_{t,p,g}|$ (Sensitivity `S6` inspects `all_nontarget`; `S5` restricts to top-75% expressed/variable genes, `4,611/6,148`). Defining true residual $r^{\mathrm{obs}}_p = \Delta^{\mathrm{obs}}_{t,p} - \hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_p$ and predicted residual $r^{\mathrm{pred}}_p = \hat{\Delta}_{t,p} - \hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_p$, pooled ordinary least squares (OLS) yields `slope_ols` and raw `resid_corr` ($r_{\mathrm{resid}}$). Because scalar rescaling along $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_p$ leaves residuals collinear with $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_p$, primary **`r_orth_cal`** orthogonally projects both $r^{\mathrm{obs}}_p$ and $r^{\mathrm{pred}}_p$ perpendicular to $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_p$ *within each perturbation's masked gene vector* prior to pooling, and disattenuates by the orthogonalized split-half Spearman–Brown noise ceiling $c^{\perp}_{\mathrm{ceil}} = \sqrt{2\rho^{\perp}_{AB}/(1 + \rho^{\perp}_{AB})}$, recomputed inside every bootstrap resample (`noise_ceiling_r_orth` computed separately on split-half vectors orthogonalized per perturbation).
- **Confidence intervals & confirmatory passage criteria:** Following `R13` Bonferroni family adjustment across Group A and Group B ($\alpha/2 = 0.025$), all confirmatory tests use two-sided `97.5%` percentile CIs from `4,000` perturbation-level paired bootstrap resamples (`np.random.default_rng(20261121)`, rule `C2`). Passage required $\ge 3/3$ confirmatory lines (`ceil(0.8 * 3) = 3`) for hypotheses and $\le 0/3$ (`floor(0.2 * 3) = 0`) inferior lines on non-inferiority across **all six** aggregate metrics (control-referenced & centroid-referenced `disc_raw_panel`, `disc_raw_ownmasked`, and `de50_r`, where `pds_cosine_panel` aligns with `cell-eval2`).
  - **Group A (`H-A1`..`H-A4`):** `H-A1` slope `97.5% CI_lo > 0.13`; `H-A2` `r_orth_cal 97.5% CI_lo > 0`; `H-A3` matched `r_orth_cal` and `slope_ols` exceed all 5 mismatched covariances; `H-A4` non-inferior to `Transfer_Mean` (`97.5%` upper diff $\le 0$ vs. `Transfer_Mean`) across all 6 conventions.
  - **Group B (`H-B1`..`H-B4`):** `H-B1` slope `97.5% CI_lo > 0.13`; `H-B2` `r_orth_cal 97.5% CI_lo > 0`; `H-B3` `r_orth_cal 97.5% CI_lo > max(all 5 mismatched Var CI97.5_hi, perm100 97.5th pct)`; `H-B4` non-inferior to `Transfer_Mean` across all 6 conventions.
- **R19 Three-Tier Multiplicative Gain Decomposition:** Using residual sum of squares $\mathrm{SST}(r^{\mathrm{obs}})$ across `resp_top50` and `all_nontarget`, variance explained is partitioned hierarchically into:
  - **Tier 1 (`L1`, Whole-line gain $\beta_t$):** Pooled affine scaling $\Delta^{\mathrm{obs}}_{t,p,g} \approx \alpha_t + \beta_t \hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_{p,g}$, yielding $L_1 = R^2_{\mathrm{tier1}}(r^{\mathrm{obs}})$.
  - **Tier 2 (`L2`, Gene-wise incremental gain $m_{t,g}$):** For genes observed across $\ge 5$ distinct perturbations in the target mask, within-gene OLS fit against $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}_{p,g}$ with cluster-degrees-of-freedom bias subtraction (`2 df` exact finite-sample $W^2$ correction across bootstrap multiplicities in `audit_runner.py`), quantifying $L_2 = R^2_{\mathrm{tier2}} - R^2_{\mathrm{tier1}}$.
  - **Tier 3 (`L3`, Unexplained line-specific residual):** $L_3 = 1 - L_1 - L_2$.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Rank-1 Whole-Line Amplitude Confounding (`R18` Callout)

Across both development and held-out lines, simply multiplying `Transfer_Mean` by one scalar per cell line (`Transfer_Mean_GlobalScale`) generates substantial raw residual correlations whenever $\beta_t \neq 1$ (**Table 2**). ## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Explaining the VCC 2026 Line-Specific Bottleneck (`0.02–0.13`) Through Pre-Registered Nulls

Our confirmatory audit directly illuminates why cross-line generalization reported in the **Arc VCC 2026 Wrap-Up** (`2026-11-19`) plateaued at slopes of `0.02–0.13` versus `1.02` for replicates:
1. **Whole-line gain confounding and `TMVS` failure:** Un-orthogonalized residual correlations (`r_cal`) and OLS slopes silently absorb whole-line scalar gain differences (`Transfer_Mean_GlobalScale`: `r_resid = -0.1263` to `+0.2501` confirmatory; `+0.3034` to `+0.5546` dev). Even when target marginal gene variance (`TMVS`) reproduces modest slope (`+0.0357` on HepG2, inside `[0.02, 0.13]`) and positive `r_orth_cal` (`+0.0419`, `+0.0947`), **gene permutations and mismatched cell-line variances match or beat matched target variances** (`H-B3: 0/3`). One possible explanation is that multiplying $\hat{\Delta}^{\mathrm{TMean}}$ by generic high-variance gene weights captures shared perturbation-variance alignment across human cell types rather than target-line specificity (`Gains over Transfer_Mean may be explained by target-line marginal gene variance`).
2. **Comparison with `CASTER` (Kawsar et al., 2026):** `CASTER` demonstrated that at `0%` target perturbation coverage, context-conditioned models drop below `PerturbMean` (`Transfer_Mean`) and require `2.5%–30%` target perturbations to surpass it. Our `H-A4` and `H-B4` failures (`3/3` inferior to `Transfer_Mean` on control- or centroid-referenced perturbation discrimination across zero-shot covariance and variance scaling) confirm that replacing `Transfer_Mean` with noisy zero-shot control-statistic perturbations harms discrimination when target perturbation responses are unobserved.
3. **Establishing the cross-line zero-shot boundary of `CIPHER v2` (Kuznets-Speck et al., 2025):** Whereas `CIPHER` succeeds within context (or across studies of the same cell type) by fitting per-perturbation response scale $\hat{a}_g$ on observed responses, constraining $\hat{\lambda}$ strictly from source lines yields chance discrimination (`CIPHER_Linear` `ctrl_disc_ownmasked = 0.472–0.483`), and augmenting `Transfer_Mean` with off-diagonal `C_t` co-fluctuations yields zero confirmatory gain beyond target marginal variance (`R14`: `-0.0044` on `vcc2025_h1`). In this dataset, co-fluctuation structure exhibits no detectable increment beyond marginal variance.
4. **Comparison with `Molina & Zhang (2026)` and prior Tier-1 amplitude metrics (`CGC`, `AMM-SimWMag`, `Systema`):** Our negative `H-A`/`H-B` findings reinforce Molina & Zhang's observation that linear regression on control means fails to recover perturbation-by-line interaction terms $\gamma_{c,p}$. Crucially, however, `R19` diagnoses a structural distortion omitted by purely additive two-way ANOVA ($\delta_{c,p} = \mu + \alpha_c + \beta_p + \gamma_{c,p}$): across all non-target genes (`all_nontarget`, free of gene-selection bias), **multiplicative whole-line scaling $\beta_t$ (`L1 = 13.1%–34.8%` on confirmatory lines, up to `46.3%` in K562 and `51.3%` in KOLF2.1J) and gene-wise gain `m_{t,g}` (`L2 = 3.7%–7.3%`) are misattributed to interaction $\gamma_{c,p}$** by additive subtraction (in the `resp_top50` subset, where genes are selected by observed $|\Delta|$ and therefore carry `S6` selection bias, `L2` rises to `14.1%–18.9%`). While `CGC` (Huang et al., 2026) and single-stimulus `AMM-SimWMag` (Sun et al., 2026) previously separated Tier-1 scalar amplitude $\beta_t$ ($T - \beta_t S$ / $\hat{s}_h$), and `Systema` (Viñas Torné et al., 2025) addressed additive systematic shifts, in our bounded literature search (`PRIOR_ART_A`–`C`) we found no study that extended amplitude separation across the three-tier multiplicative hierarchy or demonstrated how multiplicative gain inflates un-orthogonalized VCC residual correlations.
5. **Convergence with locked representation-transfer audits & `n_cells` entanglement (Shoeibi & Yousefi, 2026):** Our confirmatory NOT CONFIRMED outcome complements Shoeibi & Yousefi (`arXiv:2608.00152v3`), who under an independently locked pre-registered protocol reported that frozen Geneformer representations fail external zero-shot CRISPRi transfer (`Spearman rho = -0.139` and `-0.267`, below random-feature controls) and showed that VCC aggregate endpoints correlate strongly with perturbation cell counts (`R^2 = +0.4325` for count-only linear models vs. `+0.2589` for magnitude scalars). Together, both locked pre-registration audits caution against interpreting uncalibrated or in-distribution perturbation scores as transferable cross-line biological specificity.
6. **Prior-art boundary (`R16/R20`):** Following independent prior-art verification (`PRIOR_ART_A.md`–`C.md`), `TMVS` is framed strictly as a parameter-free variance-ratio variant of expression-aware transfer (distinct from `CellularAI` and `AtlasShift` mean-expression fold-change scaling, `CASTER` learned gating, `HyperMap` [Dhaka et al., 2026], and non-zero-shot `Luan et al.` [2026] centered kernel transfer), claiming no algorithmic novelty over those systems or Tier-1 $\beta_t$ orthogonalization in `CGC`/`AMM-SimWMag`, and recognizing Shoeibi & Yousefi (2026) as prior locked pre-registered CRISPRi evaluation.

## 6. Limitations

Seven structural and methodological limitations govern interpretation of this study:

1. **Same-model agent execution (`S1`):** Both implementation agent `A-T2` and audit agent `A-T3` belong to the same model family (`Antigravity` / `Google Gemini`). Master controller `Claude` (`Anthropic`) wrote independent verification code that replicated Group B `TMVS` `r_orth_cal` identically across the three confirmatory lines (`nadig_hepg2` `+0.0419`, `nadig_jurkat` `+0.0947`, `vcc2025_h1` `-0.0772`), while not independently recomputing Group A.
2. **Non-independence on the frozen 3-dimensional projection (`B1`):** `A-T3`'s independent audit (`audit/audit_runner.py` and `audit/independent_predictors.py`) replicated the exact same 3-dimensional subspace projection (`span{1, b_S, a_S}`) written in `src/predictors.py`, rather than implementing the literal rank-1 `q_1` projector stated in `PREREG_A v4` prose; consequently, `A-T2` and `A-T3` were not independent with respect to this projection choice (`Section 2.4`).
3. **Unpublished external VCC 2026 slope estimator:** Audit inspection of `ArcInstitute/cell-eval2` (`v0.22.0`) confirmed that the cross-line `0.02–0.13` slope formula cited in Arc's `2026-11-19` Wrap-Up is absent from public scoring code, and Dobin's metrics preprint (`10.64898/2026.11.17.767214`) remained unindexed across bioRxiv, Crossref, OpenAlex, EuropePMC, and PubMed APIs as of `2026-11-22`. Our OLS slope comparisons against `0.13` (`H-A1`, `H-B1`) therefore depend on prediction standard-deviation ratios (`0.106–0.371`), alongside Deming slopes (`slope_deming`), warranting re-checking once Arc releases the official estimator.
4. **Small confirmatory cohort (`K_conf = 3`):** Requiring unanimous `3/3` passage (`ceil(0.8 * 3)`) with `97.5%` Bonferroni CIs prioritizes false-positive control at the expense of sensitivity if one line exhibits atypical signal-to-noise properties.
5. **Low shared reproducible target count in `vcc2025_h1` (`N = 80`):** Only `80` of `300` H1 targets satisfied both Addendum-B reproducibility (`204/300`) and source-line overlap, widening bootstrap CIs (`H-A2`, `H-B2`, `R14`, and exploratory `FCT` slope all failed on `vcc2025_h1`) and making H1 sensitive to negative per-gene variance ratios under `TMVS` (`r_cal = -0.4447`).
6. **Upward selection bias in `resp_top50` noise ceilings (`S6`) & unavailable VCC 2026 test lines:** Ranking evaluation genes by observed $|\Delta^{\mathrm{obs}}_{t,p,g}|$ inflates orthogonalized split-half agreement ($\rho^{\perp}_{AB}$ drops from `0.8745 -> 0.4747` on HepG2, `0.8465 -> 0.3765` on Jurkat, `0.9885 -> 0.8886` on H1 across `all_nontarget`). Furthermore, live GCS inspection (`2026-11-22`) confirmed `gs://arc-institute-virtual-cell-atlas/virtual-cell-challenge/2026/` contains zero files and `AC16`/`MOLM13`/`U-87MG` perturbation ground truth remains unreleased; prospective Round 10 validation of `FCT`'s exploratory de-amplituded signal must therefore use open-license independent human CRISPRi datasets rather than relying on unreleased VCC 2026 data.
7. **Absence of explicit perturbation cell-count stratification (`n_cells` entanglement):** As highlighted by Shoeibi & Yousefi (2026, `arXiv:2608.00152v3`), perturbation endpoints in CRISPRi screens can correlate substantially with per-perturbation cell counts (`n_cells`) and knockdown strength. While our primary cohort required `n_cells >= 25` and Addendum-B reproducibility (and `S1` expanded to `>= 10` cells), `PREREG_A v4` did not incorporate `n_cells`-tertile stratification or partial correlation controlling for `log(n_cells)` and target-gene knockdown depth; these cell-count controls are explicitly added (`S7`) in our planned prospective Round 10 validation.
