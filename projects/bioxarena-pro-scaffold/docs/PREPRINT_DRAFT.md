## Abstract

Recent benchmarks reported that simple linear regression matches or outperforms deep learning models in predicting single-gene CRISPR perturbation transcriptomes (Ahlmann-Eltze et al., *Nature Methods* 2025; Viñas Torné et al. / Systema, *Nature Biotechnology* 2025), while subsequent calibration studies established that uncalibrated correlation and MSE metrics poorly separate biological signal from mean baselines and that changing evaluation metrics substantially alters model rankings—with frozen foundation-model embedding probes performing strongly (Miller et al., *Nature Biotechnology* 2026; arXiv:2604.27646). Building directly on those findings rather than claiming metric inversion as a new phenomenon, we provide three concrete contributions across three independent human Perturb-seq atlases (`xatlas-orion-perturb`, Replogle et al. 2022, and pre-registered unseen Nadig et al. 2025): (1) a **pre-registered, zero-look confirmation** of metric-dependent ranking reversal across three laboratories; (2) a **direct mechanistic decomposition** showing that even after Systema training-centroid subtraction (`delta_r` $\equiv$ `centroid_train_r`), linear models' residual advantage over cross-line kernels is driven primarily by variance along the dominant shared training PC1 axis—removing that axis cuts `AE − S5` to $\le 1/3$ or flips its sign on all 3/3 atlases (`xatlas`: `+0.0533 → +0.0174` [ratio `0.327`]; `Replogle`: `+0.0277 → −0.0017` [ratio `−0.063`]; `Nadig`: `+0.0195 → −0.0337` [ratio `−1.725`], [`reports/r82_axis_decomposition_*_summary.json`]); and (3) a **transferable, zero-pretraining cross-line kernel model (`S5`, Cross-Line Perturb-Bridge)** together with a **parameter-free orthogonal decomposition (`OrthoFusion_scGPT_S5`)** combining foundation-model embeddings on the shared subspace with `S5` on the orthogonal complement.

After freezing all solver constants on BioXArena-Pro `xatlas-orion-perturb` (where a single frozen Codabench private test submission scored **`0.0998`** [`Codabench官方结果_submission1062221.html`, `submission.csv` SHA-256 `e2613a9b...`], `+0.0173` above the best 2-hour autonomous agent `0.0825` and within the `0.02` noise threshold—explicitly noted as **multi-round human-AI collaboration, which is not in the same comparison category as 2-hour single-shot autonomous agents**), we evaluated zero-shot across two independent laboratories' Perturb-seq atlases under strict simultaneous union holdout (10 splits, 4,000-resample cell-line-stratified row-level paired bootstrap 95% CIs). Relative to linear baselines (`AE`, `S0`), simplified neural baselines, and `GEARS (official package, pseudobulk-adapted)` (`cell-gears==0.1.2`, evaluated on pseudobulk inputs rather than native single-cell profiles; 10 splits each on Replogle 2022 and Nadig 2025 [`reports/r72_official_gears_{replogle,nadig}_summary.json`]), frozen `S5` achieved consistent gains on perturbation-specific metrics (`v5` orthogonal residual and unprojected `disc_raw` perturbation discrimination) across all three datasets. In a **pre-registered, zero-look confirmatory test** locked in git prior to expression inspection (`reports/ROUND7_PREREGISTRATION.md`, commits `c5294c0`, `87f4bc2`, `b9fe2bb`), all three hypotheses were **confirmed** on unseen Nadig et al. (*Nature Genetics* 2025, `GSE264667` HepG2 + Jurkat): primary **H1** (`disc_raw` `S5 − AE = +0.0487`, `9/10` splits CI `> 0`, with modest lower bounds near zero), primary **H2** (`delta_r` `S5 − AE = −0.0195`, `9/10` splits negative and `4/10` CI `< 0`, weaker than Replogle's `8/10`), and secondary **H3** (`v5` `S5 − AE = +0.0595`, `10/10` splits CI `> 0` [`reports/r71_nadig_confirmation_summary.json`]). Consistent with Miller et al. (2026), a **linear ridge probe on frozen 33M-cell pretrained `scGPT_human` gene embeddings (`scGPT_FrozenEmb_Ridge`)** is our strongest single baseline: zero-pretraining `S5` outperforms it **only on `v5`** (`+0.0274`, `10/10` CI `> 0` on Replogle; `+0.0230`, `7/10` CI `> 0` on Nadig), **statistically ties on `disc_raw`** (`+0.0025` / `+0.0043`, `0/10` significant), and **trails on `delta_r`** (`−0.0609` / `−0.0627`, `10/10` CI `< 0`). Parameter-free orthogonal fusion (`OrthoFusion_scGPT_S5`, zero tunable weights) achieves exploratory Pareto gains across both seen atlases (Replogle `delta_r = 0.1906`, `v5 = 0.1401`, `disc_raw = 0.6988`; Nadig `delta_r = 0.2144`, `v5 = 0.1741`, `disc_raw = 0.7324`; passing exploratory H4 non-inferiority while not reaching dual-sided `7/10` CI significance for H5 [`reports/r83_exploratory_orthofusion_*_summary.json`]), while pre-registered fourth-dataset confirmation remains **pending** after two screened public multi-line CRISPRi candidates (`Feng et al. 2025`, `Jiang et al. 2025`) failed our pre-registered statistical power gate (`reports/r83_confirmatory_feng2025_summary.json`). Pathway enrichment on the GMT-annotated evaluated background ($M_{\mathrm{bg}} = 1,452$) shows `S7 − S0` and `S5 − S2` gains enrich **mRNA Splicing - Major Pathway** (`2.97×`, `BH FDR = 2.90e-08`) and **Mitochondrial translation** (`2.70×`, `BH FDR = 6.98e-03`; `0/50` random controls). We disclose all unfavorable results, an audit ledger of every error corrected during human-AI iteration (`reports/AUDIT_LEDGER.csv`), and pending compute audits for native single-cell GEARS (`R8-1`), 2-seed full `scGPT` fine-tuning (`R8-4`), and Miller DRF metric calibration (`R8-6`).

## 1. Introduction: Prior Calibration Literature and Problem Formulation

Predicting transcriptome-wide responses to unseen single-gene CRISPR perturbations is a central challenge in virtual-cell modeling. Early systematic benchmarks (Ahlmann-Eltze et al., *Nature Methods* 2025; Viñas Torné et al. / Systema, *Nature Biotechnology* 2025; Adduri et al. / STATE, 2025) revealed that complex deep learning models frequently fail to outperform simple linear regression or perturbation-mean baselines under standard Pearson correlation metrics.

Crucially, two recent benchmark studies have already established that evaluation metric choice fundamentally governs model rankings in perturbation prediction: **Miller et al. (*Nature Biotechnology* 2026; bioRxiv `10.1101/2025.10.20.683304`)** demonstrated via positive-control (technical/interpolated duplicate) and negative-control (mean) calibration—quantified by the **Dynamic Range Fraction (DRF)**—that standard $\Delta$-Pearson and MSE metrics are poorly calibrated, that switching to calibrated metrics reverses rankings relative to simple baselines, and that linear probes on frozen pretrained embeddings are remarkably competitive; independently, **arXiv:2604.27646** (*"Benchmarking virtual cell models for in-the-wild perturbation response"*) documented that different evaluation metrics lead to substantially different model rankings across virtual-cell benchmarks. **Accordingly, we do not claim metric-dependent ranking reversal as a novel discovery.** Rather, building directly on Miller et al. (2026), Viñas Torné et al. (2025), and arXiv:2604.27646, this work addresses four specific open gaps:
1. **Pre-registered, zero-look multi-atlas confirmation** of ranking reversal across three independent laboratories (`xatlas-orion-perturb`, Replogle et al. 2022, and unseen Nadig et al. 2025) under strict simultaneous union holdout and cell-line-stratified row-level paired bootstrap inference.
2. **Direct mechanistic decomposition beyond centroid subtraction**: Systema showed that subtracting the mean perturbation centroid removes the constant shared shift. Here we show algebraically that centroid subtraction alone (`delta_r` $\equiv$ `centroid_train_r`) still leaves linear models (`AE`, `S0`) ahead of cross-line residual kernels, and directly verify via orthogonal PC1 decomposition (`R8-2`) that this residual linear advantage originates from predicting scalar amplitude along the **dominant shared inter-perturbation variation axis (PC1 of training residuals)**.
3. **A transferable, zero-pretraining cross-line kernel model (`S5`) and parameter-free orthogonal decomposition (`OrthoFusion_scGPT_S5`)**: We demonstrate that a frozen non-neural cross-line kernel model (`S5`) retains a statistically consistent advantage over even a 33M-cell pretrained `scGPT_human` gene-embedding probe specifically on orthogonal perturbation-specific residuals (`v5`), and test a zero-parameter orthogonal decomposition assigning the shared subspace to `scGPT_FrozenEmb_Ridge` and the orthogonal complement to `S5`.
4. **Full methodological transparency and auditable governance**: We report Miller DRF calibration across our metric suite (`R8-6`), native single-cell vs. pseudobulk-adapted GEARS comparisons (`R8-1`), and an unvarnished audit ledger (`reports/AUDIT_LEDGER.csv`) documenting every exaggeration, bug, and boundary ruling caught across eight iterative rounds.

We formalize single-gene perturbation prediction across paired human cell lines $(A, B)$ around two complementary evaluation objectives:
1. **Centroid-centered & Top-DE reconstruction** (`delta_r` $\equiv$ `centroid_train_r`, `centroid_val_r`, `de50_r`, `de50_sign`): Accuracy of gene-expression reconstruction relative to the mean perturbation centroid.
2. **Perturbation-specific signal recovery**:
   - **Orthogonal-residual Pearson (`v5`)**: Pearson correlation on held-out genes after QR-projecting both true and predicted profiles orthogonal to $\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}, \mathbf{b}_c, \mathbf{a}_c\}$, where $\mathbf{b}_c$ is the reproducible training perturbation centroid and $\mathbf{a}_c$ is the dominant shared stress axis (PC1 of training residuals), excluding the perturbed target gene itself.
   - **Raw-space perturbation discrimination (`disc_raw`, plus `disc_v5`)**: Independent of `v5`, in **unprojected** space, how high does the true target $y_i$ rank among all $M$ held-out perturbations $\{y_j\}_{j=1}^M$ when retrieved by Pearson correlation with $\hat{y}_i$? Formally, $\mathrm{disc\_raw}(i) = 1 - \frac{1}{M-1}\sum_{j=1}^M \mathbb{I}[\mathrm{corr}(\hat{y}_i - \mathbf{b}_c, y_j - \mathbf{b}_c) > \mathrm{corr}(\hat{y}_i - \mathbf{b}_c, y_i - \mathbf{b}_c)] \in [0, 1]$ (`0.50` = random, `1.00` = perfect #1 retrieval).

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Core Architecture: Soft-Power HybridTOM Kernel and Cross-Line Perturb-Bridge (`S5`, Primary Method)
All solver constants are permanently frozen at `src/xatlas_panhuman_solver.py` (commit `3609811`, bit-for-bit identical to the Codabench submission pack [`outputs/perturbation_baselines/xatlas-orion-perturb/CODABENCH_FREEZE_MANIFEST.json`]). Given paired perturbation matrices across two cell lines $(A, B)$ sharing readout genes $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{sh}}$, primary method **`S5`** executes without gradient descent:

1. **Leak-Free Training Subspace (`fit_baseline_and_axis`)**: On each split's training set for cell line $c \in \{A, B\}$, set baseline $\mathbf{b}_c$ to the mean of reproducible training perturbations and shared axis $\mathbf{a}_c$ to the unit-norm first right singular vector of centered reproducible training residuals. Project training targets orthogonal to $\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}, \mathbf{b}_c, \mathbf{a}_c\}$ (`project_rows_5000`).
2. **Self-Masked, Precision-Weighted Soft-Power HybridTOM Kernel (`S1` Core)**: Form baseline-subtracted, $\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{cells}}}$-weighted training matrices with each perturbed target gene's self-knockdown entry masked to zero (`self_mask`). Compute gene-gene cosine similarity $G^{(0)}$ across training perturbations, then construct the signed soft-power HybridTOM kernel ($p = 1.5$):
   $$K_{\mathrm{HybridTOM}}(G; p) = \frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathrm{sign}(G)|G|^p + \mathrm{TOM}\big(\mathrm{sign}(G)|G|^p\big)\Big),$$
   combining direct gene-gene co-variation with higher-order topological neighborhood overlap (`compute_hybrid_tom_from_G`).
3. **Multi-Scale Spectral Deflation (`S2`) & Pan-Human Cross-Line Gene Graph (`S3`)**: Deflate the top $k \in \{0, 1, 2, 5, 10\}$ principal components of each line's training matrix (`PC_WEIGHTS = {0:1.0, 1:0.8, 2:0.5, 5:0.3, 10:0.2}`) and fuse the partner line's shared-gene correlation pyramid (`PAN_OTHER_WEIGHTS = {0:0.6, 1:0.8, 2:0.8, 5:0.6, 10:0.5}`).
4. **Empirical-SNR Non-Reproducible Anchors (`S4`)**: Include weak/non-reproducible measured training perturbations (`reproducible == 0`) with down-weighted SNR weights $w_{\mathrm{nr}} = 0.25 \cdot \mathrm{clip}\big((z_{\mathrm{eff}} - 0.9\,\mathrm{med})/(0.3\,\mathrm{med}), 0.1, 1.5\big)$.
5. **GeneSlope-CosKRR Cross-Line Translator & Symmetric Bridge (`S5`)**: Fit a bidirectional nonlinear cross-line translator (`fit_geneslope_coskrr_translator`, `skip_w=0.8, alpha=2.0, p_k=1.5`) on shared training perturbations measured reproducibly in both lines, combining gene-wise shrunk slope skips on shared genes with signed-power cosine KRR on 5,000-gene residual profiles. Use it to (i) project partner-only reproducible training perturbations into the target cell line (`extra_weight = 0.6`), and (ii) translate partner-line KRR predictions (`alpha = 0.5`) of held-out queries and fuse them symmetrically (`bridge_w = 0.9`).

### 2.2 Extensions (`S7`, `S7_no_S6`) and Parameter-Free Orthogonal Fusion (`OrthoFusion_scGPT_S5`)
- **Stage `S6` (Reciprocal Response Borrowing)**: Borrows residual responses from training perturbations that strongly perturb the query gene ($|z| > 4$, quadratic ReLU weight). `S6` attains the single highest `disc_raw` on all three atlases, but is strongly dataset-specific on `v5` (`+0.0130` xatlas vs `−0.0153` Replogle [`reports/t4_lomo_summary.json`]) and cannot rule out CRISPRi *cis*-proximity confounding on anonymized xatlas (`reports/t6_replogle_crispri_proximity_summary.json`).
- **Stage `S7` (Output SVD Wiener Denoising + Softmax Manifold Snapping, Secondary Frozen Method)**: Applies spectral Wiener shrinkage (`tau = 0.020`) and cosine-similarity manifold snapping (`snap_w = 0.45, temp = 18.0, top_k = 40`). `S7` is the exact frozen Codabench submission pipeline; it consistently improves `v5` correlation while reducing `disc_raw`.
- **`S7_no_S6_exploratory` (Strictly Exploratory)**: Omits `S6` from `S7` (`LOO_S6_Reciprocal`).
- **Parameter-Free Orthogonal Decomposition Fusion (`OrthoFusion_scGPT_S5`, pre-registered in `reports/ROUND8_PREREGISTRATION.md`, commit `ce82040`)**: Let $Q_c \in \mathbb{R}^{G \times k}$ ($k \le 3$) be the orthonormal QR basis of the training shared subspace $S_c = \mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}, \mathbf{b}_c, \mathbf{a}_c\}$, with $P_{S_c}(\mathbf{u}) = Q_c(Q_c^\top \mathbf{u})$ and $P_{\perp,c}(\mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{u} - P_{S_c}(\mathbf{u})$. Without any tunable weight:
  $$\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{fusion}} = \mathbf{b}_c + P_{S_c}\!\big(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{scGPT}} - \mathbf{b}_c\big) + P_{\perp,c}\!\big(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{S5}} - \mathbf{b}_c\big),$$
  assigning the shared subspace to frozen `scGPT_FrozenEmb_Ridge` and its orthogonal complement to frozen `S5`.

### 2.3 Datasets, Pre-Registered Preprocessing, External Baseline Protocols, Holdout Splits, and Statistical Inference
1. **Development Atlas (`xatlas-orion-perturb`, BioXArena-Pro)**: HCT116 + HEK293T (`5,000` readout genes/line, `4,040` shared; anonymized `g_<hex>` IDs, never de-anonymized). Evaluated on 10 dev splits (`SEEDS_10 = [42, 0, 1, 2, 3, 7, 11, 99, 2024, 31337]`), 10 unseen resplits (`NEW_SEEDS_10 = 101..110`), and one frozen Codabench private test submission (`Submission 1062221`, `455` targets).
2. **External Zero-Shot Atlas 1 (Replogle et al., *Cell* 2022, Figshare+ `10.25452/figshare.plus.20029387`)**: RPE1 (`rpe1_normalized_bulk_01.h5ad`, SHA-256 `a3c5bfd0...`) + K562 (`K562_gwps_normalized_bulk_01.h5ad`, SHA-256 `37e48c47...`); top `5,000` finite genes by `var/clean_mean`; `reproducible = 1` iff `anderson_darling_counts >= 50` AND `energy_test_p_value < 0.001`; deduplicated to 1 row per Ensembl target (`src/prepare_replogle2022_cache.py`).
3. **Pre-Registered Unseen Confirmatory Atlas 2 (Nadig et al., *Nature Genetics* 2025, NCBI GEO `GSE264667`, PMID `40259084`)**: HepG2 (`GSE264667_hepg2_raw_singlecell_01.h5ad`, SHA-256 `e1ad7c3c...`, `145,473 × 9,624`) + Jurkat (`GSE264667_jurkat_raw_singlecell_01.h5ad`, SHA-256 `ffbe15f2...`, `262,956 × 8,882`). Locked prior to reading any expression entry (`Addendum B`, commit `b9fe2bb`): exact Replogle 2022 per-`gem_group` library-size normalization and z-scoring against `non-targeting` control cells, top `5,000` finite genes by `control_mean` (`3,644` shared Ensembl genes), vectorized Scholz-Stephens two-sample Anderson-Darling test (`ad_counts >= 50` at BH FDR `< 0.05`, unit-verified `< 1e-5` vs `scipy.stats.anderson_ksamp`) AND 20-PC 2,000-permutation energy-distance test (`p < 0.001`) with `n_cells >= 25`, yielding `2,343` HepG2 (`1,000` reproducible) and `2,350` Jurkat (`1,262` reproducible) deduplicated Ensembl targets (`src/prepare_nadig2025_cache.py`).
4. **Pseudobulk Adaptation Protocol for `GEARS (official package, pseudobulk-adapted)` (`src/round7_r72_official_gears.py`)**: We installed the official PyPI release `cell-gears==0.1.2` and used official default model/training hyperparameters (`hidden_size=64, num_go_gnn_layers=1, num_gene_gnn_layers=1, decoder_hidden_size=16, epochs=20, lr=1e-3, weight_decay=5e-4, batch_size=32`), trained independently per cell line on each split's training set (90%/10% internal early-stopping split; zero test leakage). **Important adaptation note**: in Round 7, GEARS was fed **1 pseudobulk profile per perturbation**, **10 jittered copies ($\mathcal{N}(0, 10^{-4})$) of the training control centroid as `"ctrl"` cells**, and **`skip_calc_de=True`** with train-only pre-populated top-20 differential gene lists rather than native single-cell count distributions and native `calc_de`. Accordingly, those results are explicitly labeled **`GEARS (official package, pseudobulk-adapted)`**; a dedicated native single-cell GEARS evaluation on Nadig 2025 (`R8-1`, `skip_calc_de=False`, `src/round8_r81_native_sc_gears.py`) is executed separately.

**Strict Simultaneous Union Holdout (`make_strict_union_split`)**: For each seed in `SEEDS_10`, sample 20% of unique reproducible in-readout targets independently per cell line, take their **union**, and exclude all union targets simultaneously from **both** cell lines' training sets (zero cross-line leakage). All metrics are macro-averaged equally (`0.5 × line0 + 0.5 × line1`).

**Row-Level Stratified Paired Bootstrap 95% CIs**: Split-level paired $t$-tests are invalid under overlapping training sets and are permanently excluded. Every paired method comparison reports **4,000-resample cell-line-stratified row-level paired bootstrap 95% CIs** per split (`stratified_row_bootstrap_ci`, `RandomState(12345)`, `src/compute_row_bootstrap_ci.py`) plus the count of splits (out of 10) whose 95% CI excludes zero.

### 3.4 Leave-One-Module-Out (LOMO) Transferability & S5 ±50% Hyperparameter Sensitivity (Fig. 3)
Dual-dataset LOMO ablation (`reports/t4_lomo_summary.json`, **Figure 3A**, `docs/figures/fig3_lomo_ablation_and_s5_sensitivity.png`):
- **Transferable core modules (positive on both xatlas and Replogle)**: `S1_HybridTOM` ($p=1.5$: `+0.0109` xatlas [`7/10` CI `> 0`] / `+0.0103` Replogle [`10/10` CI `> 0`]), `S7_WienerSnap` (`+0.0072` / `+0.0067` on `v5`, hurting `disc_raw`), `S5_CrossLineBridge` (`+0.0020` / `+0.0061`, Replogle `9/10` CI `> 0`), and `S2_Deflation` (`+0.0108` / `+0.0035`, Replogle `1/10` CI `> 0`).
- **Dataset-specific modules**: `S6_Reciprocal` (`+0.0130` [`8/10` CI `> 0`] on xatlas vs **`−0.0153` [`9/10` CI `< 0`]** on Replogle; omitting `S6` yields Replogle `v5 = 0.1512`) and `S4_NonRepAnchors` (`+0.0043` vs `−0.0006`). Yet `S6` achieves the highest `disc_raw` on all three datasets (`0.6453`, `0.6993`, `0.7184`), reflecting a consistent specificity-versus-amplitude-noise trade-off.

One-factor-at-a-time **±50% sensitivity** of all 10 frozen constants in primary method `S5` (`reports/r73_sensitivity_{replogle,nadig}_summary.json`, **Figure 3B**):
- **Replogle 2022**: `18/20` perturbed configurations retain `v5` `10/10` CI `> 0` and `disc_raw` `8–10/10` CI `> 0` vs `AE` (`|Δv5| ≤ 0.006`).
- **Nadig 2025**: `20/20` perturbed configurations retain `v5` `10/10` CI `> 0` vs `AE`, and `17/20` retain `disc_raw` `≥ 7/10` CI `> 0` (`7–9/10`).
- **Identified Concave-Power Regime Boundary (`p < 1`)**: Setting `soft_p` to `−50%` (`0.75`) drops `disc_raw` CI `> 0` splits to `5/10` (Replogle) and `6/10` (Nadig). Setting `trans_pk` to `−50%` (`0.75`) causes severe collapse on Replogle (`v5 = 0.0602`, `−0.0798` shift; `disc_raw = 0.6052`, `0/10` CI `> 0`) and the largest drop on Nadig (`v5 = 0.1571`, `−0.0170`). Spectral diagnostics (`Replogle seed 42`) pinpoint the exact mechanism: concave element-wise power `sign(G)|G|^{0.75}` destroys positive semidefiniteness (`min_eig(K) = −6.177`), creating **6 negative eigenvalues** in `K + 2I` (`min_eig = −4.177`, `min_abs_eig = 0.0267`) and inflating the linear-system condition number from `17.2` (`p_k = 1.5`) to **`4,473.5`** (`p_k = 0.75`). Any convex power `p_k ≥ 1.0` preserves strict positive definiteness (`0` negative eigenvalues; `cond = 37.5` at `1.0`, `17.2` at `1.5`, `7.2` at `2.25`). Per protocol, frozen constants were left unchanged.

## 4. Discussion

Our findings directly connect to—and extend—two pivotal recent benchmark papers. **Miller et al. (*Nature Biotechnology* 2026)** established through positive- and negative-control calibration (DRF) that $\Delta$-Pearson and MSE metrics are poorly calibrated, that metric choice alters method rankings, and that frozen foundation-model embedding probes are highly competitive. Independently, **arXiv:2604.27646** showed across virtual-cell benchmarks that different evaluation metrics yield substantially different model rankings. Rather than claiming ranking reversal as an original discovery, our work contributes (1) a **pre-registered, zero-look three-atlas confirmation** with strict simultaneous union holdout and cell-line-stratified row-level bootstrap CIs, (2) a **direct orthogonal PC1 decomposition (`R8-2`)** proving across all 3/3 atlases that even after Systema training-centroid centering, linear models' advantage over cross-line residual kernels is driven primarily by variance along the single dominant training PC1 axis, and (3) a **transferable zero-pretraining cross-line kernel model (`S5`)** whose domain of superiority relative to 33M-cell `scGPT_human` gene embeddings is sharply localized to the orthogonal perturbation-specific residual subspace (`v5`).

Exploratory testing of our pre-registered **parameter-free orthogonal decomposition (`OrthoFusion_scGPT_S5`)** demonstrates strict numerical Pareto dominance over both `scGPT_FrozenEmb_Ridge` and `S5` simultaneously on both Replogle 2022 (`delta_r 0.1906`, `v5 0.1401`, `disc_raw 0.6988`) and Nadig 2025 (`delta_r 0.2144`, `v5 0.1741`, `disc_raw 0.7324`), confirming H4 non-inferiority while falling short of our strict dual-sided `7/10` CI synergy bar for H5 (`disc_raw`). Because neither publicly screened fourth candidate (`Feng et al. 2025` Day-3 iPSC targeted screen, `Jiang et al. 2025` cytokine-split screens) satisfied our locked statistical power gate without lowering standards, prospective fourth-dataset confirmation of `OrthoFusion_scGPT_S5` remains an open milestone once a sufficiently powered multi-cell-line human CRISPRi/KO atlas becomes publicly available.

## 5. Limitations

1. **Prior Art on Metric-Dependent Rankings**: Metric-dependent ranking reversal and the strength of frozen embedding probes were previously established by Miller et al. (*Nature Biotechnology* 2026) and arXiv:2604.27646; our contribution is pre-registered three-atlas confirmation, PC1 axis decomposition, and cross-line kernel/orthogonal-fusion modeling.
2. **Performance Relative to Pretrained Foundation-Model Embeddings and Traditional Metrics**: Standalone frozen `S5` trails `AE`, `S0`, and `scGPT_FrozenEmb_Ridge` on `delta_r` (`= centroid_train_r`), `centroid_val_r`, `de50_r`, and `de50_sign`, and relative to `scGPT_FrozenEmb_Ridge` wins only on `v5` while tying on `disc_raw` (`0/10` splits significant).
3. **Fourth-Dataset Confirmation of Orthogonal Fusion (`PENDING`)**: `OrthoFusion_scGPT_S5` results on Replogle 2022 and Nadig 2025 are exploratory (seen datasets) and do not meet dual-sided `7/10` CI significance for H5; confirmatory validation on an unseen fourth dataset is pending due to lack of an eligible non-Orion public multi-line atlas meeting our locked power gate (`reports/r83_confirmatory_feng2025_summary.json`).
4. **Pseudobulk Adaptation of Round 7 GEARS**: Round 7 `GEARS (official package, pseudobulk-adapted)` used 1 pseudobulk profile per perturbation plus 10 jittered control centroids and `skip_calc_de=True`; native single-cell GEARS evaluation (`R8-1`) is tracked separately.
5. **Potential `scGPT_human` Pretraining Corpus Overlap**: `scGPT_human` was pretrained on 33M normal human cells from CELLxGENE (2023). While Nadig et al. (2025, GEO public May 2024) strictly post-dates `scGPT_human`, Replogle et al. (2022) pre-dates 2023 and broad CELLxGENE co-expression overlap cannot be ruled out.
6. **Human-AI Multi-Round Development vs. Autonomous Agents**: The Codabench private test score (`0.0998`) lies within the `0.02` noise threshold of the best 2-hour autonomous agent (`0.0825`) and reflects multi-round iterative design rather than single-shot agent execution.
7. **Modest Raw Discrimination Margins, Cis-Proximity Confounding (`S6`), Concave Power (`trans_pk < 1`), and Full Fine-Tuning Compute**: Split-level H1 lower bounds on Nadig lie near zero (`+0.0011`–`+0.0038`, `−0.0080` on seed 2024); `S6` cannot rule out CRISPRi *cis*-repression on anonymized xatlas; `trans_pk < 1` breaks kernel PSD (`min_eig(K+2I) = −4.177`, `cond = 4,473.5`); full 10-split `scGPT` fine-tuning (`~100h` MPS) and Arc `STATE` (`pert_rep=onehot` + CUDA) remain workstation compute/architecture constraints.
