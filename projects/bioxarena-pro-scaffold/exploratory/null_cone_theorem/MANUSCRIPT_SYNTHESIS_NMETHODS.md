## Abstract

Evaluating whether virtual cell models predict cell-line-specific CRISPRi perturbation responses—rather than recycling cross-line averages—remains a central bottleneck in single-cell foundation modeling. Across three commit-locked, blinded pre-registered studies spanning **13 human Perturb-seq cell lines (`10,334,369` QC single cells)**, we audited covariance transfer (`Hybrid_ProjShared`), marginal-variance scaling (`TMVS`), and baseline-driven fold-change transfer (`FCT`). Under pre-registered confirmatory protocols, neither `Hybrid_ProjShared` nor `TMVS` was confirmed (Round 9, `PREREG_A v4`, `K_conf = 3`, `0/4` criteria), `FCT` passed amplitude-removed correlation (`H-C1`, `2/2`) and non-inferiority (`H-C3`, `12/12`) yet failed cell-line specificity (`H-C2`, `0/2` under `resp_top50`, Round 10 `PREREG_C`, `K_conf = 2`), and prospective genome-wide validation (`PREREG_D`, Round 11) was non-confirmatory due to insufficient eligible lines (`K_conf = 1`, `cd4t_stim48`). Unexpectedly, Round 11's pre-specified out-of-sample split-half DEG control (`SA-8`, `resp_top50_split`) revealed that **split-half gene selection—proposed by Nicol, Shivakumar & Irizarry (2026) to cure finite-sample control-noise inflation—fails to eliminate spurious cell-line-specific scores**, with target-free constant-baseline predictions (`E1`) and scrambled-gene permutations (`Perm100`) remaining strongly positive across **`12/12`** eval-valid human cell lines (`E1(SA-8) = +0.0529` to `+0.2859`; `Perm100` median `+0.0289` to `+0.1916`; paired `E1(SA-8) − E1(all)` `97.5% CI > 0` on `10/12` lines). We derive the deterministic mechanism (`Theorem 1`): `log1p(CP10k)` imposes a monotone baseline Jacobian $w(\mu) = \mu/(1+\mu)$, so conditioning on large target deltas $|\Delta_t| \approx w(\mu_t)|\delta|$ enriches $w_t \to 1$ genes and induces an infinite-sample $\delta^2$-weighted intercept $a_{M,\delta^2} > 0$ that split-half noise removal purifies rather than removes. To resolve this without using any target-line information, we prove (`Theorem 2`) that the entire family of baseline-free `log1p` fold-change transforms across six orders of magnitude ($c \in [10^{-3}, 10^3]$) is captured at `99.987%` variance (`min R^2 = 0.99919`) by three fixed target-free nodes $c \in \{0.05, 1.0, 20.0\}$. Projecting out the target-free five-dimensional null cone $\mathcal{N}_5 = \mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}, \Delta_{\mathrm{TMean}}, b_S, g_{0.05}, g_{1.0}, g_{20.0}\}$ via semi-partial calibrated correlation (`r_cone_sp`) collapses all four benchmark artifacts across all `48` `(12 lines × 4 masks)` evaluations (`GlobalScale`: `0.0000`; `Source_Grand_Mean` $b_S$: `0.0000` from `[-0.28, +0.45]`; `E1`: `[-0.0002, +0.0001]` from `[-0.09, +0.29]`; `Perm100` median: `[-0.0043, +0.0056]` from `[-0.06, +0.19]`) while retaining orthogonal energy $\eta = 0.745\text{–}0.934$ on true `FCT` (`0.996` on `Hybrid_ProjShared`). Under `N_5` whole-transcriptome evaluation (`post hoc`), `FCT` exceeds `E1` (`97.5% CI > 0`) and `Perm100 Q97.5` on `12/12` lines and exceeds the worst mismatched cell-line control (`CI97.5_hi`) on `8/12` lines (`8/9` lines with `N >= 90`), consistent with our `33`-pair conservation analysis (`Q5a`) showing that noise-calibrated perturbations are more conserved in `logFC` than `log1p` space on expressed genes ($\mu \ge 0.5$: `25/33` pairs, Wilcoxon `p = 1.47e-03`) and that target-mapped `FCT` outperforms direct `log1p` transfer on `33/33` pairs (`p = 2.33e-10`). Nonetheless, target-baseline scaling explains only `~0.3%–3.6%` of orthogonal residual variance (`r_cone_sp = +0.0567` to `+0.1899`), underscoring that `>95%` of cell-type-specific regulatory rewiring remains unsolved.

## 1. Introduction

Zero-shot prediction of transcriptional responses to genetic perturbation in unseen human cell types has emerged as a flagship testbed for virtual cell models. Yet recent independent evaluations—including the Arc Institute Virtual Cell Challenge 2026 Wrap-Up (top-10 cell-line-specific slopes `0.02–0.13` vs biological replicates `1.02`), `CGC` (Huang et al. 2026), `Molina & Zhang` (2026), and `CASTER` (Kawsar et al. 2026)—demonstrate that contemporary foundation models largely recover cross-line average responses (`Transfer_Mean`) while failing to capture genuine cell-line-specific residuals.

Measuring whether any candidate mechanism truly adds cell-line-specific signal turns out to be fraught with subtle evaluation traps. Previous work identified finite-sample shared-control noise amplified by in-sample top-$k$ DEG selection (Nicol, Shivakumar & Irizarry 2026, `doi:10.64898/2026.05.07.723486`), existence-threshold sign leakage (`cell-eval2 #351`), perturbation-invariant systematic offsets (`Systema`, Viñas Torné et al. 2025), and scalar amplitude confounding (`AMM-SimWMag`, Sun et al. 2026; our Round 9 `R18`). Crucially, Nicol et al. (2026) showed that splitting cells into disjoint selection and evaluation halves collapses zero-change `ctl-mean` false positives back to zero (`-0.044` to `+0.047`), leading the field to treat out-of-sample split-half DEG evaluation as a complete remedy.

Here, through three commit-locked blinded audits across **13 human Perturb-seq atlases (`10,334,369` single cells)** followed by exact mathematical analysis, we show that split-half DEG selection **does not cure** a distinct, deterministic nonlinear bias inherent to `log1p(CP10k)` expression spaces. We then construct and validate a **zero-target-data universal null-cone evaluation framework (`N_5`)** that eliminates all four classes of spurious residual scores simultaneously, and provide the direct multi-line test (`Q5a`) of whether perturbation biology is more conserved in multiplicative (`logFC`) or additive (`log1p`) space.

## 3. Mechanistic Resolution & Universal Null-Cone Cure (Post-Hoc Synthesis Across 13 Lines)

### 3.1 Theorem 1: Why Out-of-Sample Split-Half Top-DE Selection Fails on `12/12` Human Cell Lines (`Figure 1A–B`, `Figure 2`)

In `log1p(CP10k)` space, a conserved multiplicative perturbation $\mathrm{FC}_{p,g} = \exp(\delta_{p,g})$ produces $\Delta_{c,p,g} = \log(1 + \mu_{c,g} e^{\delta_{p,g}}) - \log(1 + \mu_{c,g}) = w(\mu_{c,g})\delta_{p,g} + \kappa(\mu_{c,g})\delta_{p,g}^2 + O(\delta^3)$, with Jacobian $w(\mu) = \mu/(1+\mu) \in [0, 1)$ (`THEOREM_AND_PROOFS.md`). After legacy 1D orthogonalization (`N_1`, removing $\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}, \Delta_{\mathrm{TMean}}\}$), the inner product between the target residual and any target-free log-fold-change predictor ($E6 = \delta$, or $E1(c) \approx w(c)\delta$) is governed by the deterministic $\delta^2$-weighted intercept $a_{M,\delta^2} = \mathbb{E}_{M,\delta^2}[w_t] - \beta_{1,M}\mathbb{E}_{M,\delta^2}[\bar{w}_S]$.

Conditioning on large target deltas $|\Delta_t| \approx w(\mu_t)|\delta|$ enriches genes near $w_t \to 1$, compressing $\mathrm{Var}_M(w_t)$ while $\bar{w}_S$ regresses toward its mean and forcing $a_{M,\delta^2} > 0$ even at **zero sampling noise** (`Figure 1A–B`). Removing sampling noise via split-half selection (`SA-8`) eliminates noisy false top-$k$ genes and purifies the high-$w_t$ signal tail.

Across all **12 eval-valid human Perturb-seq cell lines** (`N = 9..1,300`; `n_boot = 4,000`, `n_perm = 100`, `seed = 20261124`; `RESULTS_13LINE_NULL_CONE_AUDIT.md` Section 2):
- Under `N_1`, `SA-8` out-of-sample split-half selection increases baseline-free `E1` relative to `all_nontarget` on **`12/12` lines** (`97.5% CI > 0` on `10/12` lines; only the two `N = 9` lines `calu3` and `telohaec` have wide CIs spanning `0`), reaching `+0.0529` (`cd4t_stim48`) to `+0.2859` (`vcc2025_h1`), and equals or exceeds in-sample `resp_top50` on `7/12` lines (`Figure 2A`).
- Simultaneously, `Perm100` medians jump from `[-0.0616, +0.0354]` (`all`) to **`[+0.0289, +0.1916]`** (`SA-8` split-half) across `12/12` lines (`Figure 2B`).
- In a pure-noiseless cross-benchmark audit (where split-half is mathematically identical to in-sample; `Figure 1B`), target-free `E6` scores `+0.7176` under `CGC`/`Molina` top-50 residual correlation and `+0.6556` under `N_1` top-50 `r_orth`, while `E1` scores `+0.4641` and `Perm` scores `+0.2710`.

### 3.2 Theorem 2: The Universal Target-Free Null Cone `N_5` Annihilates All Four Benchmark Artifacts (`Figure 1C`, `Figure 3`)

Every constant-baseline transform (`E1`, `E2(P10/P90)`), asymptotic fold-change limit (`E6`, `E7`), and expected permutation null ($\mathbb{E}_\pi[\mathrm{FCT}(\mu_\pi)]$) lies inside the source-only convex cone $\mathcal{C}(\overline{\mathrm{FC}}_p) = \overline{\mathrm{conv}}\{g_c(\overline{\mathrm{FC}}_p) = \log(1 + c\,\overline{\mathrm{FC}}_p) - \log(1+c) : c > 0\}$. Over six orders of magnitude ($c \in [10^{-3}, 10^3]$), just three fixed target-free nodes $c \in \{0.05, 1.0, 20.0\}$ span `99.987%` of cone variance (`min R^2 = 0.99919`, `median R^2 = 0.99985`; `Figure 1C`).

Adding the source-only perturbation-invariant grand mean $b_S$ (which otherwise scores up to `+0.4478` under legacy `N_1` despite zero target or perturbation specificity) yields the **100% target-free five-dimensional null cone**:
$$\mathcal{N}_5(p) = \mathrm{span}\Big\{\mathbf{1},\; \Delta_{\mathrm{TMean},p},\; b_S,\; g_{0.05}(\overline{\mathrm{FC}}_p),\; g_{1.0}(\overline{\mathrm{FC}}_p),\; g_{20.0}(\overline{\mathrm{FC}}_p)\Big\}.$$

Using semi-partial normalization $r_{\mathrm{cone\text{-}sp}} = c_{\mathcal{N}_5}^{-1} \langle P_{\mathcal{N}_5}^\perp r_{\mathrm{obs}}, P_{\mathcal{N}_5}^\perp r_{\mathrm{pred}}\rangle / (\|P_{\mathcal{N}_5}^\perp r_{\mathrm{obs}}\|_2 \|(r_{\mathrm{pred}})_c\|_2)$ prevents `0/0` re-amplification of $10^{-3}$ Taylor curvature tails. Across all **48 `(12 lines × 4 masks)`** real human evaluations (`Figure 3A–B`, `RESULTS_13LINE_NULL_CONE_AUDIT.md` Section 4):
- `GlobalScale`: **`[-0.0000, +0.0000]`** ($\eta = 0.0000$).
- `Source_Grand_Mean` ($b_S$): drops from `[-0.2795, +0.4478]` to **`[-0.0000, +0.0000]`** ($\eta = 0.0000$).
- `E1_ConstMed`: drops from `[-0.0948, +0.2866]` to **`[-0.0002, +0.0001]`** (median $\eta = 0.0012$).
- `E2_ConstP10` / `E2_ConstP90`: drop from `[-0.0995, +0.3004]` to **`[-0.0003, +0.0002]`** (median $\eta = 0.0006\text{–}0.0025$).
- `E6_LogFCBar`: drops from `[-0.1033, +0.2986]` to **`[-0.0003, +0.0011]`** (median $\eta = 0.0054$).
- `Perm100` median: drops from `[-0.0616, +0.1916]` to **`[-0.0043, +0.0056]`** (on `SA-8` split-half: `[-0.0027, +0.0023]`).
- True target-specific predictors suffer negligible attenuation: on `DLD-1`, `Hybrid_ProjShared` retains $\eta = 0.996$ and synthetic true regulatory rewiring retains $\eta > 0.995$ (`test_null_cone.py::test_4`), while `FCT` retains $\eta = 0.745\text{–}0.934$ on `all_nontarget` (`Figure 3B`).

Under de-biased `N_5` whole-transcriptome evaluation (`all_nontarget`, `Figure 3C`), `FCT` achieves positive `97.5% CIs` exceeding `E1` (`FCT − E1` `97.5% CI > 0`) and exceeding `Perm100 Q97.5` on **`12/12` lines**, and exceeds the strict upper bound of all mismatched cell-line control means (`FCT CI97.5_lo > max(MaxMis CI97.5_hi, Perm100 Q97.5)`) on **`8/12` lines** (`8/9` lines with `N >= 90`: `nadig_hepg2`, `nadig_jurkat`, `replogle_k562_essential`, `replogle_rpe1`, `vcc2025_h1`, `dld1`, `cd4t_rest`, and `feng_ipsc`—resolving Round 10 `cd4t_rest`'s previous `0.002` miss to `+0.0394 > +0.0378`). Only `cd4t_stim48` (`+0.0596` vs `vcc2025_h1 CI97.5_hi +0.0766`, despite `17×` separation above `Perm100 Q97.5 +0.0035`) and the three small-`N` lines (`N = 9..20`) have overlapping mismatched-control bounds.

### 3.3 Direct Q5a Cross-Line Conservation Test Across 33 Human Cell-Line Pairs (`Figure 4`)

Evaluating all **33 pairs** of human cell lines sharing $\ge 25$ reproducible perturbations (`RESULTS_13LINE_NULL_CONE_AUDIT.md` Section 5; verified novel in `PRIOR_ART_D.md` Q5a):
- In **raw uncalibrated correlation**, `logFC` loses to `log1p` (`0/33` pairs on `all` and `expr01`, `5/33` on `expr05`) because low-expression division amplifies shot noise (`+0.1394` vs `+0.1915` on `all`).
- After **split-half noise-ceiling calibration**, conservation reverses on expressed genes (`Figure 4A`): at $\mu \ge 0.5$ (`expr05`), true `logFC` conservation exceeds `log1p` (`+0.3566` vs `+0.3466`) on **`25/33` pairs** (paired Wilcoxon `p = 1.47e-03`).
- Transporting conserved `logFC` into the target cell line's `log1p` compression (`FCT`) captures multiplicative conservation while restoring low-expression noise damping, outperforming direct `log1p` transfer on **`28/33`** (`all`, Wilcoxon `p = 1.28e-05`), **`32/33`** (`expr01`, `p = 2.94e-07`), and **`33/33`** (`expr05`, `p = 2.33e-10`; `Figure 4B–C`) pairs.

## 4. Discussion and Honest Limitations

1. **Practical Guidance for Virtual Cell Benchmarks (`VCC 2026`, `cell-eval2`, `Systema`, `scPerturBench`):** Benchmarks evaluating cell-line-specific generalization in `log1p` space should (i) discontinue unadjusted top-DE correlation—even with split-half selection—as a standalone test of specificity, (ii) replace 1D residual metrics with the target-free `N_5` semi-partial cone metric (`null_cone_metrics.py`), and (iii) pair whole-transcriptome (`all_nontarget`) or target-blind source-selected (`top50_src`) masks with mismatched-line and permuted-baseline controls.
2. **Biological Reality Check:** While target-baseline `log1p` rescaling (`FCT`) is a real, reproducible non-additive mechanism missed by additive linear probes (`Molina & Zhang 2026`, `CASTER 2026`), its de-biased residual correlation across 12 cell lines is `r_cone_sp = +0.0567` to `+0.1899` (`R^2 ≈ 0.3%–3.6%`). Over `95%` of genuine cell-type-specific transcriptional rewiring remains unexplained by baseline expression geometry alone.
3. **Post-Hoc Demarcation & Prospective Path:** Rounds 9–11 remain locked as originally adjudicated (`NOT CONFIRMED` / `K_conf = 1`). The `N_5` cure and `Q5a` analysis are post-hoc discoveries derived after Round 11 unblinding; prospective pre-registration of `N_5` on additional unseen single-sgRNA lineages (optional Round 12) is straightforward using our frozen, unit-tested `null_cone_metrics.py` library.
4. **Scope Boundaries:** Results apply to CRISPRi pseudobulk `log1p(CP10k)` perturbations; evaluation of deep neural perturbation foundation models directly under `N_5` (requiring public model weights trained on matched source cohorts) and extension to single-cell distributional metrics (`edistance` / Wasserstein) are natural next steps.
