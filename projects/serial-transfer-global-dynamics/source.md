# Primary sources, comparison and limits

Last inspected: 27 October 2026. A bounded primary-source search found no exact equivalent of the proposed full-positive-basin argument in the passages below. This is not a priority guarantee or an exhaustive literature review.

1. **Sebastian J. Schreiber (2025).** *Coexistence and extinction in flow-kick systems: An invasion growth rate approach*. Journal of Mathematical Biology 91:50. [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-025-02283-0); [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02157v2), revised 1 September 2025. The current journal Sections 2.2, 3.3, 5.2 and 6.2 were inspected. Equation (26) and its following paragraph leave the all-positive-initial-composition exclusion implication open; the full-basin issue also appears in the discussion. The model and invasion criteria are Schreiber's presentation of the Yi–Dean serial-transfer system. The growth/threshold/common-dilution model in Section 2.2 is used directly.
2. **Xiao Yi and Antony M. Dean (2013).** *Bounded population sizes, fluctuating selection and the tempo and mode of coexistence*. PNAS 110:16945–16950. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1309830110); [primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3801030/). The Theory section derives rare-type invasion and coexistence conditions for constant within-environment growth and density-triggered dilution. No all-positive-basin proof was found in the inspected main text. NCBI metadata reports no supplement; no supplementary document was established or falsely represented as inspected.
3. **Xiao Yi (2015).** *Ecology and evolution in fluctuating environments*, University of Minnesota doctoral thesis. [Institutional PDF](https://conservancy.umn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/80ee5c32-2612-4fc6-bd86-cb5a82df4bc8/content). Chapter 2, printed pages 8–10 and 18–24, was inspected as additional author material. It develops the related seasonal/serial-transfer theory; no equivalent full-basin proof was found in those passages. This thesis is not a 2013 supplement.
4. **Antony M. Dean (2005).** *Protecting Haploid Polymorphisms in Temporally Variable Environments*. Genetics 169:1147–1156. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.104.036053); [primary text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1449120/). The variable-environment section after equations (22)–(23) explicitly confines its analysis to rare clones, excluding interior dynamics far from quasi-steady state. Indexed primary sections were inspected; several formulas are images, so a complete equation-level PDF comparison is not claimed.
5. **Hal L. Smith (2011).** *Bacterial competition in serial transfer culture*. Mathematical Biosciences 229:149–159. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mbs.2010.12.001). The publisher's indexed introduction, model and result overview were inspected. This uses prescribed transfer time, a dynamic resource and lag phases; the complete theorem-level PDF was not obtained. Deeper overlap with classical serial-transfer theory therefore remains a search limitation.
6. **Stacey R. Smith?, Tyler Meadows and Gail S. K. Wolkowicz (2024).** *Competition in the nutrient-driven self-cycling fermentation process*. Nonlinear Analysis: Hybrid Systems 54:101519. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nahs.2024.101519); [author-hosted PDF](https://rsmith43.github.io/mysite/competition.pdf). The first author's question mark follows the published author name. System (3.3), Theorem 2 and Corollary 1 were inspected. They give prior global alternatives for a nearby two-species nutrient-triggered no-death fermentation model, with fixed resource-response functions. An equivalence to alternating external per-environment growth rates has not been established. Global state-triggered competition classification in general is not new.

Older Stewart–Levin and global-chemostat theorem texts were not fully screened. An existing hypothesis-preserving reduction to another global theorem would change the novelty assessment. No absence-of-prior-work conclusion follows from a failed download or a missing repository. No paper-specific Schreiber code repository was established by the inspected author-page/GitHub search; an unrelated project is not treated as an upstream destination.

Only original explanation, code and certificates are included here. Third-party source PDFs/HTML, private correspondence and contact records are not redistributed.
