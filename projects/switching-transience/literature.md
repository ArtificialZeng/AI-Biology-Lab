leaves Example 44/Conjecture 46 unresolved. No exact resolution was found in the

not a priority certificate; unpublished author work was not checked.

Branching processes in random environments are classical. Smith and Wilkinson,
*On branching processes in random environments* (1969),
[DOI10.1214/aoms/1177697589](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177697589), give a
logarithmic-mean survival criterion. Our proof instead supplies the needed
conditional second-moment survival argument directly, so an inaccessible copy
of that classical theorem is not a proof dependency. No new general branching
theorem is claimed.

The source cites Kaplan(1973),
[DOI10.2307/1425963](https://doi.org/10.2307/1425963), and Key(1987),
[DOI10.1214/aop/1176992273](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176992273).
Kaplan's publisher abstract concerns continuous-time extinction criteria; its
full theorem hypotheses were not obtained, so full coverage is unverified.

[DOI10.1214/009117906000001015](https://doi.org/10.1214/009117906000001015),
was checked in that primary paper's Assumption1.1/Theorem2.1. Its stated setting
is subcritical; it does not furnish the present transience calculation. Other
inaccessible parts of Key are not asserted to have been excluded.
