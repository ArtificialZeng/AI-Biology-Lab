# Exact scope

Consider the literal proportional-recruitment/standard-incidence variant of the uniform-susceptibility class in Earn–McCluskey (2025): recruitment is nu*N entirely into S, the force of infection is beta^T*x/N, susceptible mortality is muS, and postinfection transitions form the source PIT matrix M. All postinfection classes are included in N; no class returns to S. Infection entry has one fixed probability vector P independent of the transmitting class. All postinfection nodes must be reachable from infection entry; transfer rates may be zero. Every mortality is positive; infectivities are nonnegative and not all zero. Put N=S+sum(x), s=S/N and y=x/N.

The original witness has nu=34/5, muS=5, M=diag(1,17), P=(1/10,9/10), beta=(24,1). Its fraction field has a positive saddle, a disease-free sink and another positive sink. Its below-susceptible mortality mu1<muS is permitted by the source's literal domain. The [original proof](proof.md) and its two exact calculations remain available.

The [addendum](natural-mortality.md) also gives a model with mu_j>=muS for every postinfection class and two sinks plus a positive saddle. A different exact witness satisfies mu_j>muS for every postinfection class, strictly positive infectivities and both transfer rates, and has a positive saddle (and a disease-free sink). No second positive sink is certified for this strict witness. Thus mortality ordering alone, even with these strict rates, does not ensure a single globally asymptotically stable fraction equilibrium. No full equilibrium count or basin classification is asserted.

This corrects the scope of the unqualified Discussion section “Proportional scaling”; it does not refute the main constant-recruitment theorem. The mortality-order question is a question formulated for this audit, not an open conjecture attributed to the source authors. Fraction equilibria are balanced-growth states; the positive equilibria used in the addendum have positive growth. No real-world disease or oscillation claim is made.

Equal mortality in every compartment is one sufficient repair: normalization produces the original constant-recruitment class with recruitment and every effective mortality equal to nu. Necessity and a complete classification remain unclaimed. The ambient two-infective family and bistability are known from Razvan–Yasaman (2012); novelty of the exact joint restrictions is not certified.
