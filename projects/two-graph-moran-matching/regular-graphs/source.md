Its model §§2.1–2.3 uses neutral birth–death replacement on simple undirected connected graphs, with a parent's type determining which graph supplies its uniformly chosen neighbor. Its fixation statistic averages a uniformly chosen singleton. Section3.4 shows that different regular mutant graphs can have different fixation probabilities against complete residents. AppendixA.1 proves the single-deleted-edge result. AppendixB.1 gives a particular complete-bipartite lower bound. Discussion's first question asks whether every noncomplete mutant graph is disadvantaged against complete residents, and conversely whether complete mutants are advantaged against every noncomplete resident graph. Those broader arbitrary-graph statements are not solved here.
